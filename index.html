About

I study how energy systems change under climate policy, and what those changes demand from global trade and material supply chains.

I am a PhD student in the Department of Geographical Sciences at the University of Maryland, advised by Professor Laixiang Sun. My work couples the Global Change Analysis Model (GCAM) with material flow analysis, decomposition methods and scenario analysis to ask questions such as: which critical minerals become bottlenecks as clean electricity is built out for AI data centers, when U.S. liquefied natural gas actually reduces emissions abroad, and how the steel industry can reach net zero.

Before UMD, I earned an M.S. in Information Systems from Johns Hopkins University and a B.Mgt. in Business Administration from Zhejiang University of Technology, and worked in data analysis and consulting.

Research

AI data centers and critical minerals

The clean electricity bought to power AI data centers is built from solar panels, wind turbines, grid connections and batteries, and these draw on minerals mined and refined in a few countries. I couple GCAM with dynamic material flow analysis for the U.S., China and Europe to trace which materials data center growth depends on through 2050, and when and where supply becomes tight.

GCAM, material flow analysis, Monte Carlo uncertainty, Shapley decomposition

LNG trade and emissions

Whether U.S. LNG lowers emissions abroad depends on methane leakage, shipping distance and what it displaces. I estimate breakeven leakage thresholds against coal in European and Asian markets, project LNG trade flows to 2050, and use LMDI decomposition and counterfactuals to measure how coal-to-gas switching and supply disruptions change the carbon intensity of gas use.

GCAM, LMDI decomposition, scenario and counterfactual analysis

Steel decarbonization and circular economy

I model global steel transition pathways under net-zero scenarios, including hydrogen-based DRI and electric arc furnaces, and use material flow analysis to estimate how far scrap recycling can close the gap between rising steel demand and carbon constraints.

GCAM, material flow analysis, technology pathway analysis
